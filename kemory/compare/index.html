How Kemory compares,
including where it doesn't win.

Living Brain, Supermemory and Kemory all promise one memory that works across your AIs. They get there in different ways, and the differences show up in the first ten minutes: whether your chats arrive on their own, whether you can pick up a conversation in another AI, and who else can read what you have stored.

Everything said here about another product comes from that product's own public site, documentation or pricing page, as they stood on 5 November 2026, and each claim links to its source at the foot of the page. Products change quickly. If something here is out of date or wrong, email hello@sekondbrain.ai and we will correct it.

The short version

Living Brain is a memory API that compiles what your AIs send it into tidy, versioned pages, and it marks a page as contested when a new fact contradicts an old one. It is built for developers: memory is saved when a model decides to call its capture tool, or when your own code sends it chat turns.

Supermemory is a fast, open-source memory API with MCP and plugins. In September 2026 it discontinued its consumer app, its browser extension and its company-brain product to concentrate on the API, so it is now a choice for developers rather than for people capturing their own chats.

Kemory gathers your chats on its own through Kora for Chrome, lets you continue a conversation in another AI, asks several models one question with Counsel, and lets you share memory with other people, including people in other organisations, with every cross-organisation read recorded in an audit log. Access is deny-by-default at every tier.

Why the benchmark numbers do not line up

All three publish a headline score, and all three are in the high 80s or 90s. They measure different things on different datasets, so putting them side by side tells you very little.

ProductPublished figureWhat it measures
Kemory89.6% on LongMemEval-S, oracle setEnd-to-end answer accuracy: retrieve, answer with an open-weight model (Llama-3.3-70B), judge against the reference. Six runs published with their spread
Living Brain92.2% on LoCoMo (home page); 90.5% in its documentationAnswer accuracy on LoCoMo, a different and smaller conversational benchmark; the documented run answers with GPT-5
Supermemory97% on LongMemEval-SRecall@20: whether the evidence appeared in the top 20 results, not whether the final answer was right

Our figure is on the harder axis, end-to-end accuracy with an open model, but it is on the oracle set, which contains only the sessions that hold the evidence. We have not run either product on an identical harness, so no ranking between the three is proven, including one that would flatter us. The benchmark method page explains the difference between retrieval and end-to-end figures in full.

Choosing between them

Choose Living Brain if

You are a developer who wants a readable, self-organising wiki behind your own agents, you are happy to decide in code when to save, and you value seeing contradictions laid out as contested pages.

Choose Supermemory if

You want a fast, open-source memory API to wire into your own product today, and team access and compliance on its Scale plan suit your budget.

Choose Kemory if

You want your chats gathered without remembering to save them, to pick up a conversation in another AI, several models answering one question, or memory shared with other people, with a door between you, an audit trail, and data held in Switzerland or India on every plan.
